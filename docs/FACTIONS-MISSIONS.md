# Browser factions and missions

The public browser profile now exposes all sixteen countries in the RTS AI catalog: America, Germany, England, France, Korea, Cuba, Libya, Iraq, Russia, China, Iran, Türkiye, Saudi Arabia, Israel, Yemen and Hezbollah. Random faction pools include the restored countries.

The nine restored countries retain the mod's original unit rules, weapons, costs and signature units. Their browser rendering uses existing project-owned faction artwork and announcers. Shared infrastructure uses the standalone base kit. These are playable gameplay rosters; they do not introduce nine new bespoke art sets. Automated checks cover deployment, power production, technology prerequisites, signature availability, unit and structure creation, deployed GI/Desolator states, icons and snapshot rendering. They do not establish competitive balance.

## Missions

The launch menu offers Skirmish and Missions. Mission cards show a briefing, map preview, objectives and difficulty; in-game objectives include progress and target location buttons. Victories are recorded locally by difficulty.

Five missions are adapted from authored companion content:

- Jizan Corridor, Hodeidah Lifeline and Bab al-Mandab Passage: `OpenRA-AI/missions/red-sea-2026`.
- Haitan Network: `OpenRA-AI/missions/china-faction`.
- Straits Shield: `OpenRA-AI/missions/turkey-faction`, with the source map binary recovered from the Classic OpenRA packaged map.

Their source maps use the older RA terrain and actors. The browser ports preserve terrain layout, resources, important locations and core objectives, while replacing terrain tiles and units with compatible standalone RA2 project content. Objectives run in the deterministic simulation host because the browser build does not link the native Lua mission runtime. Relief vehicles and shipping likewise use available project units. These are adaptations, not byte-identical campaign imports.

Relay Under Siege is a new fictional Hezbollah training exercise, built on Twin Fords. No authored Hezbollah mission was found. It tests faction infrastructure, defense against waves and an attack objective. Old Classic campaign maps and internal AI fixtures are not exposed as compatible playable RA2 missions.

Two original chapters of Lines of Hope, Act III, are generated by `prepare-content.py` (`chapters()`) on the terrain of two adapted maps, with every actor placed in code. The generator checks each placement: buildings on land and off beaches and ore, ships and ferry waypoints on water, and a land route from Orion's wave entry to each target. Orion Group is the fictional contractor force; like the Obsidian Directorate in Haitan Network it uses the `russia` faction (unit art and the scripted wave type) and colour 545D66. The scripted waves (`Missions.Wave`) attack the player's oldest building still standing, so the order of the buildings in the generator sets where Orion strikes first.

- **Lights in the Water** (`lights-in-the-water`, Yemen, Captain Nabil Haidar): Hodeidah Lifeline's terrain with a reef coast painted along the south edge. A mobile force of 14 with no base must bring three units to each of three stranded groups (`SurvivorsCove`, `SurvivorsJetty`, `StrangerSite`; the stranger is the actor `Stranger`, on a non-combatant Survivors side whose original-country faction gives her the original commando art), each guarded by an Orion patrol (rifles, desolators, Tesla tanks), with a roadblock on the coast road. The transports can cross the reef only at high tide (a `survive` objective, 8:00); then three transports drive from `ReefLanding` along the coast road to `ClinicDoor`, and two must arrive. Meanwhile Orion's columns grind down the `WatchPost` line and then the town: left alone, the clinic (`TownClinic`) falls at about 12 minutes. Losing the clinic or every unit fails the mission. Map rules: the town's defences and generators do not count as the force, Orion's wave tanks (`htnk`) have 260 HP, and the transports (`sapc`) are slow (speed 50).
- **Hold the Ferry Line** (`hold-the-ferry-line`, Türkiye, Colonel Kemal Aydın): Straits Shield's terrain. A small port base with $10,000 holds for 12 minutes. The columns take `NorthCheckpoint`, then `EastCheckpoint`, then `SouthCheckpoint`, then the piers (each has its own gun). Tanya (`Tanya`) is with the defenders. At 6:00 four ferries sail from the piers through the channel, past a picket of four Orion destroyers (`OrionPicket0`-`3`); every ferry must reach the far shore. Losing either pier (`FerryPierNorth`, `FerryPierSouth`) fails the mission. Map rules: the ferries (`lcrf`) are slow, unarmoured hulls (speed 50, 150 HP).

Balance on normal was set with headless runs of the real simulation (`tools/mission-probe`: it boots `OpenRA.WebSim` like `websim run`, optionally attack-moves the whole force through named actors, and prints the objectives each minute). Idle player: Lights in the Water is lost at about 12 minutes (the clinic), Hold the Ferry Line at about 6:15 (the picket sinks a ferry). A crude scripted player that attack-moves its whole force site to site, then sweeps back along the coast road, wins Lights in the Water at about 9:35 on easy and normal; one that leaves the road uncleared loses the transports. In Hold the Ferry Line the same kind of player, using only the starting units and no production, sinks the picket and gets all four ferries across; it wins on easy and loses the north pier at about 10:45 on normal, so holding to 12:00 needs the base's production. Hard is much harder in both (the scripted waves are six tanks every 34 seconds from 0:40): the scripted players lose at about 7 minutes. Human playtests remain the real check.

All eight missions are available immediately.

## Local generation and validation

From `RTSAI-WebGame`:

```powershell
python tools/prepare-content.py
dotnet build sim/OpenRA.WebSim.Native -c Release -p:WebProfile=public
tools/public.ps1 -NoBuild
& C:/Users/Admin/.dotnet/dotnet.exe publish sim/OpenRA.WebSim.Browser -c Release -p:WebProfile=public -o data/public/wasm-interp
npm --prefix web run build
node server/serve.mjs
```

Open `http://127.0.0.1:3490/?profile=public&runtime=interp`. The public profile requires no original RA2 files. Generated packs, runtime output and sprite caches are local build artifacts.

Native regression commands accept an absolute extracted VFS root:

```powershell
websim.exe contenttest --root <absolute-vfs-root> --faction america
websim.exe missiontest --root <absolute-vfs-root> --mission haitan-network --mode success
websim.exe missiontest --root <absolute-vfs-root> --mission haitan-network --mode failure
node tools/fidelity.mjs --profile public --runtime interp --scenario scenarios/mission-haitan-network.json --ticks 300
node tools/content-browser-test.mjs
```

The browser regression currently targets local port3505. Native completion tests exercise scripted objective transitions and actual convoy/amphibious routes; they remove enemy combat units to isolate objective regression from combat balance. Manual difficulty and balance playtesting remains useful.

This work is local only. The website gateway is `RTSAI-Web` (`alibad/RTSAI-Web`); `/play` forwards to the Vercel game deployment. This browser checkout has no configured Git remote. Publishing requires a separate explicit deployment request.
